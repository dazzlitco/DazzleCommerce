- Eyebrow/label: `The Problem`

- Intro: `Twelve things that turn a store into a constraint. The audit usually finds the real bottleneck is one or two of them, and the rest are symptoms.`
- 12 cards:
  1. `Slow or outdated stores` — `Apps stacked on apps and a theme three major versions behind. Every added feature makes the next one slower.`
  2. `Poor mobile experiences` — `Most of the traffic, a fraction of the revenue, on a layout that was designed and signed off on a desktop monitor.`
  3. `Limited platform functionality` — `The business needs something the platform does not do natively, and the available app does 60% of it badly.`
  4. `Difficult integrations` — `An ERP, a 3PL and a POS that each expect a different data shape, joined by a spreadsheet somebody exports weekly.`
  5. `Disconnected systems` — `Stock in one system, orders in another, customers in a third. No single source of truth, so every report disagrees.`
  6. `Checkout friction` — `Extra steps, missing payment methods and a shipping calculator that reveals cost at the worst possible moment.`
  7. `Scalability limitations` — `A build that works at 500 orders a month and falls over at 5,000, because nothing was designed for the second number.`
  8. `Technical SEO problems` — `Faceted URLs generating index bloat, broken canonicals and JavaScript rendering that hides content from crawlers.`
  9. `Frequent bugs` — `Releases that break something else each time, because there is no staging environment and no regression testing.`
  10. `Difficult store maintenance` — `Nobody left who understands the custom code, so every small change becomes a quote and a two-week wait.`
  11. `Legacy technology` — `A stack chosen five years ago for reasons nobody documented, now expensive to host and impossible to hire for.`
  12. `Platform limitations` — `A genuine ceiling on what the current platform can do, reached long before anybody admitted it was the constraint.`

  1. Q: `How long does an eCommerce build take?` A: `A straightforward Shopify build on a customised theme is typically 6–10 weeks. A replatform with data migration and integrations runs 10–16. Custom functionality or B2B portals extend that further. The variable that moves timelines most is not development speed — it is how quickly content, product data and decisions come back from your side, which we scope explicitly at planning.`
  2. Q: `What does eCommerce development cost?` A: `Project builds typically start around $15,000 for a well-specified Shopify store and run to $60,000+ for replatforms with custom functionality and integrations. Ongoing maintenance and development retainers run $1,500–$6,000/month. We scope from requirements rather than quoting a package.   3. Q: `Which platform should we be on?` A: `For most D2C brands under roughly $20M, Shopify or Shopify Plus, because the ecosystem and hosting burden are solved problems. WooCommerce where content and WordPress integration genuinely matter. BigCommerce for complex B2B pricing. Adobe Commerce only where the catalog complexity truly demands it and you have budget for the maintenance. We will tell you if your current platform is fine — migrations are expensive and often unnecessary.`
  4. Q: `Will a replatform cost us our search rankings?` A: `Not if it is planned properly. A full URL inventory, a mapped redirect for every URL that has earned a link or a ranking, preserved URL patterns where they carry equity, and a staged crawl-and-fix programme through the first eight weeks. On the Luxury Comforts migration we retained 94% of rankings. Migrations lose traffic when redirects are treated as a launch-day task rather than a planning-phase deliverable.`
  5. Q: `Do you work with our existing developer or agency?` A: `Frequently. We can take a defined workstream, work alongside an in-house developer, or act as the escalation path for work beyond their specialism. What we ask for is clear ownership boundaries in writing, because shared codebases without them produce conflicts nobody wants to untangle at launch.`
  6. Q: `What happens after launch?` A: `Either a maintenance retainer or a handover, and both are legitimate choices. A retainer covers monitoring, updates, small development and a named developer who knows the codebase. If you would rather take it in-house, we document the build and hand over cleanly — we will not hold your store hostage through undocumented custom code.`
  7. Q: `Can you fix a store somebody else built?` A: `Yes, and about half our development work starts that way. The first step is an audit of the existing codebase to establish what is salvageable, because sometimes an honest answer is that a rebuild costs less than continuing to patch. We will tell you which one it is before you commit budget.`
  8. Q: `Do you handle hosting and infrastructure?` A: `On Shopify and BigCommerce it is handled by the platform. On WooCommerce and Adobe Commerce we will specify and configure hosting, CDN and caching, and either manage it or work with your provider. We do not resell hosting, so the recommendation is based on what the store needs rather than what we margin on.`
