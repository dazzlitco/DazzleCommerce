- H2: `Our eCommerce design process`
- Intro: `Six stages. Stage one is measurement, and stage six is measurement again — without both, a redesign is an opinion with a budget.`
- 6 steps:

  2. `02` — `Research` — `Session recordings, heatmaps, on-site search queries including the ones returning nothing, support tickets, and a usability pass on your current templates on real handsets.`
  3. `03` — `Structure` — `Information architecture, navigation and wireframes agreed before any visual design exists, because arguing about layout and colour at the same time is how both decisions get made badly.`
  4. `04` — `Design` — `Templates designed mobile-first, every state included, against a design system rather than as one-off screens. Accessibility and image-weight budgets applied as we go.`

  6. `06` — `Measure & iterate` — `The baseline re-measured per template after launch, then conversion testing on what the data says to question. A redesign that is never measured cannot be defended or repeated.`

### 10. FAQs
- Eyebrow/label: `FAQs`
- H2: `eCommerce web design FAQs`
- Intro: `Eight questions that come up before most design engagements. More on our full FAQ page .` (Quirk: space before the period, kept verbatim; link text `our full FAQ page` → /faqs/)
- 8 FAQs (also in JSON-LD FAQPage, verbatim):
  1. Q: `How is this different from your development service?` A: `Design decides what the store should do and how it should behave; development makes it exist. They are usually bought together and they are genuinely different work: one produces research, architecture, templates and a design system, the other produces a working storefront, integrations and a release process. If your problem is that pages are slow or the platform cannot do something, that is development. If the pages work and customers still leave, that is this.`
  2. Q: `Do we need a full redesign?` A: `Usually not, and we will say so. Most stores we review need three or four templates rebuilt rather than a full redesign — typically category, product and checkout — because those carry the revenue and are where the research points. A full rebuild is the right answer when you are replatforming anyway, when the navigation cannot carry the catalogue any more, or when there is no system and every page is bespoke.`
  3. Q: `What does eCommerce web design cost?` A: `It depends on how many templates are in scope, whether a design system exists already, and whether research has to be done from scratch. A template programme on an existing store is the smallest piece of work here; a full replatform design is several times that; a design system on its own sits between them. We scope from the audit rather than quoting a package, and our pricing page explains the approach.` (Link: `our pricing page` → /pricing/)
  4. Q: `How is this different from hiring a design studio?` A: `A studio optimises for the work looking right, and it is usually judged at presentation. We optimise for what the templates do to conversion rate and revenue per session, because the same team runs the store afterwards. In practice that changes three things: research comes before layout, every state gets designed rather than just the happy path, and we agree the number the work will be judged against before we start.` (Quirk: British spelling `optimises` kept verbatim.)
  5. Q: `Do you build what you design?` A: `We can, and often do, but it is a separate decision. Our development team works on Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce and Adobe Commerce, and taking both halves means nobody argues about whether a handover was complete. If you have an in-house team or an existing agency, we hand over to them and we do not make that awkward — the design system is documented for exactly that case.` (Link: `development team` → /ecommerce-development/)
  6. Q: `What do you hand over?` A: `Editable source files, a design system with tokens and components, documented states for every template including empty, loading and error, accessibility annotations, and the written rationale for the decisions we made. Not a PDF of screenshots. The test we hold ourselves to is whether a developer who has never spoken to us could build it correctly from what we handed over.`
  7. Q: `Is accessibility really necessary?` A: `Yes, for two reasons. It is a legal requirement in a growing number of markets, and the exposure sits with the retailer rather than the agency. Commercially it also overlaps almost entirely with good design: contrast, target sizes, focus order, clear labels and keyboard operation help every customer on a phone in bright sunlight, not only screen-reader users. We design to WCAG 2.2 AA because retrofitting it later costs several times more than building it in.`
  8. Q: `How is a redesign measured?` A: `Against the baseline agreed before we start: conversion rate and revenue per session per template and per device, funnel progression between steps, Core Web Vitals, and an accessibility pass. We report the templates that got worse as well as the ones that improved, because a redesign almost never moves everything in one direction and pretending otherwise makes the next decision worse.`
